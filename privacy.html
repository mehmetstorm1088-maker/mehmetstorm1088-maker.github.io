Privacy policy

A simple informational website. A separate, private publishing tool for the adult channel owner.

Effective date: 15 October 2026

1. What this website does

BlinkQuest Lab introduces a planned collection of English animal cartoon stories and links to the publisher’s YouTube channel. The website does not provide visitor accounts, a contact form, Google sign-in, purchases or an upload interface.

We have not added analytics, advertising trackers, embedded YouTube players or custom cookies. The page uses local styles and original inline illustrations. This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub states that it logs and stores visitors’ IP addresses for security, including when visitors are not signed in. Its Pages documentation does not specify a retention period. See GitHub Pages data collection and the GitHub Privacy Statement for the hosting provider’s handling.

2. Information about children

This website is written for parents and caregivers. We do not invite children to submit personal information, create an account or connect a Google account. The intended age range describes the planned stories, not access to the publisher’s administrative tools. No child-facing OAuth or Google account connection is offered.

Our links open YouTube as a separate service. YouTube may process information and use cookies under its own policies when you visit it. Families should choose the service and viewing settings appropriate for their children. See Google’s Privacy Policy and YouTube’s Terms of Service.

3. The adult publisher’s Google connection

BlinkQuest Lab’s separate publishing workflow is used only by the adult channel owner. It is not available for website visitors or children to authorize. The owner connects their own Google account to publish their own videos and to verify that the workflow is using the intended YouTube channel.

  • Upload permission: youtube.upload is used to upload the owner’s video files and metadata.
  • Read permission: youtube.readonly is used to read the authorized channel’s identity and check it against the expected channel ID.
  • Information used: authorization tokens, the channel ID, upload metadata, video IDs, upload status and timestamps. The workflow does not collect individual viewer profiles or viewer analytics.

Google authorization data is used for this channel-publishing function, not advertising, sale of data or training general-purpose AI models. Access is limited to the owner and the services needed to operate the owner’s workflow. We follow the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements, for information received from Google APIs. Any use or transfer is limited to the stated publishing purpose and other uses allowed by that policy.

4. Storage, services and retention

The owner keeps local authorization files in a private working folder outside the public website and source deliverables. These local files are not encrypted by the authorization helper. The GitHub publishing environment stores its authorization configuration as an encrypted Actions secret. Credentials are not included in this website, its HTML, downloadable public source files or visitor-facing pages.

The publishing journal records channel/video IDs, original-content and media hashes, upload-request hashes, timestamps and completion states. It is stored in publisher-controlled storage, including a private GitHub state branch, to avoid duplicate uploads and recover interrupted jobs. Non-secret upload records may also be shared to document published videos. Generated video artifacts are configured for one-day retention and recovery artifacts for seven days; repository settings may change those periods. The persistent upload journal has no automatic expiry configured.

Authorization files are kept until the owner replaces or removes them. Upload records may be kept while needed for publishing history and duplicate protection. The owner controls deletion of local files, repository records, secrets and artifacts; this website does not provide an automatic data-deletion interface. Deleting local files or a GitHub secret does not itself revoke a Google grant. We do not promise a fixed automatic deletion period that has not been configured.

5. Revoking access and contacting the publisher

The owner can review and revoke the Google connection through Google Account connections. When access is no longer needed, the owner should turn off the publishing automation, revoke the grant and remove stored local authorization files and the GitHub secret. Existing YouTube videos and previously saved publishing records are managed separately.

For contact, use the public contact options, if available, on the BlinkQuest Lab YouTube channel. This website has no contact form and does not publish a separate contact email.

6. Updates

This policy may change as the website or publishing workflow changes. The effective date above will be updated when the policy is revised.
